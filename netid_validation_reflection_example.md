
# Validation Reflection — afink12

Across the ten projects validated from bxy539, agreement between author and validator was extremely high. We agreed on all HasRecovered and CurrentStatus fields, as the evidence for those is not interpretable and can be found simply by the existence of commits on certain dates. We had a few disagreements for the WhyRecovered field, but they were resolved once a discussion was held surrounding both our reasonings. ActivityPattern and RecentThemes had the most disagreements, which may be because these fields are based largely on interpretations of the data. However, these conflicts were resolved once a discussion was held.

There were some common signals that preceded long gaps. Many projects had low or no activity following version releases, which included commits for documentation, feature development, branch merges, and bug fixes. Other gaps were caused by downtime between work cycles (for projects with a cyclical commit timeline pattern). Also, some common triggers for recovery were the need for dependency updates (provoked by automated bot messages or authors themselves), fixing bugs that may have been caught after a release, or starting a new work cycle/sprint (cyclical pattern). Based on all the projects I looked at, gaps followed by a release or feature addition typically didn't lead to project death, as most repositories resumed commits after a gap due to the need to upgrade the project or update dependecies/fix bugs to keep the project at its best functionality.

One main pitfall occurred in the ActivityPattern field, mainly caused by different interpretations between irregular graphs and other categories. For instance, a commit timeline may appear to be decreasing overall, but may include many large activity spikes, which could be interpreted as either declining or irregular based on how generalized the interpreter chooses to be. These were resolved by looking back at the timeline graphs and discussing our reasonings, which led to agreements afterwards. Another main pitfall was within the RecentThemes field. There are many similarities between some of the categories, which can lead to different interpretations within the commit messages. For instance, lots of branch merges could be interpreted as cleanup for a release, or merging feature updates into the main branch. This category had many disagreements due to it largely being attributed to the interpretation of the analyst. However, these issues were resolved after we looked back at the commit messages, explained our reasonings, and had insightful discussions that led to aligned agreements.
